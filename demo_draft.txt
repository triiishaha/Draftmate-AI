LEGAL NOTICE

The conduct of the Noticee violates the rights of the Noticor. The Supreme Court held in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) 4 SCC 225 that the basic structure of the Constitution cannot be altered. In Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India, AIR 1978 SC 597, it was held that any procedure affecting personal liberty must be fair, just and reasonable. The right to privacy was affirmed in K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017) 10 SCC 1. On online speech, see Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) 5 SCC 1. On workplace safety see Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan, AIR 1999 SC 3011. Personal dignity was reiterated in Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018) 10 SCC 1.

The dishonour of the cheque is actionable under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, and damages for breach are claimed under Section 73 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. The Noticee should also note Rajesh Kumar v. Imaginary Traders Pvt Ltd (2019) 7 SCC 999.
